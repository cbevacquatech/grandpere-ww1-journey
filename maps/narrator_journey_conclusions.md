# The narrator's journey: evidence and reasoning

## Scope and method

This reconstruction follows the instruction to treat **Persons/Speakers A, B, C, D, and E as one composite narrator**. That is a working assumption for the map, not a claim that the diarization is correct: several C–E passages read like interview prompts. The route is based on first-person movement statements anywhere under those labels, while repeated tellings of the same episode are consolidated into a single stop.

The map is a **sequence map, not a precise track map**. A solid dark marker means the transcript names a place clearly. A hollow rust marker means the position is approximate, the modern identification is uncertain, or the transcript names only a region. Dashed connecting lines show narrative order; they do not establish the exact road, railway, ship route, or front line.

## How the duplicate accounts were reconciled

The transcript retells the same wartime episodes several times. Repetition was treated as corroboration when the sequence and details matched:

1. **Induction and eastward movement:** Strasbourg/training area → Tilsit → boat → narrow-gauge/open railway → march into Russian territory.
2. **First winter:** unnamed Russian farms and villages → trenches → winter quarters. These details describe movement but do not justify additional precise pins.
3. **Galicia and wound:** boxcars to Galicia → mountain/front area → trenches/orchard/woods → field hospital after the chest wound.
4. **Recovery:** field hospital → Silesia → Marienburg → recuperation company and nearby farm duty. The exact order between Silesia and Marienburg is taken from the fullest telling at `jaques-a` 00:18:14–00:20:27 and 00:29:23–00:31:55.
5. **Return:** Königsberg? → Berlin → Wiesbaden → Strasbourg → unidentified Alsace home → Strasbourg.
6. **Civilian migration:** Strasbourg → Paris → ship to New York → Manhattan, with later trips to Welfare Island, Newark, the Bronx, and Belmar.

## Important exclusions

- Joseph's Second World War movements (Hungary, Normandy, England/Camberley, France, Rhône and Rhine valleys) are **not** mapped. In that section Jacques is narrating his brother's journey, not his own.
- Oradour-sur-Glane is discussed as an event, not visited by the narrator, so it is not a route stop.
- Most unnamed Russian villages and farms, the first field hospital, Silesian hospital, recuperation town, farm-work site, and exact New York arrival pier remain unpinned. The Galician landscape episode is now represented by evidence-qualified candidates rather than treated as an entirely unknown battlefield.
- No straight connecting line should be read as proof of the actual historical route between stops.

## Newly supplied documentary records

- [S.S. *La Lorraine* manifest, original image](assets/familysearch_1922_la_lorraine_manifest.pdf), with [FamilySearch OCR transcript](assets/familysearch_1922_la_lorraine_manifest_ocr.pdf): FamilySearch image 138 of 871, image group 007619417, NARA source. Line 18 is Jacques Schneider, age 26. This record supersedes the earlier hypothesis of a 16 March 1923 arrival. The sheet documents departure from Le Havre on 18 February 1922 but does not print the New York arrival date or pier.
- [1950 West Belmar census sheet](assets/familysearch_1950_west_belmar_census.pdf): FamilySearch image 14 of 59, image group 108972570, NARA source. It corroborates Jacques's later Monmouth County residence and gives his household and occupation.

These records fit the already confirmed birth date particularly well: a person born 16 July 1895 was 26 on 18 February 1922 and 54 at the 1950 census. That double age match, together with the exact name, French/Paris origin, waiter/bartender work, Newark intention, and West Belmar family, makes the identification substantially stronger than the oral-history chronology alone.

The CompGen casualty index preserves these exact fields from the wounded record: surname Schneider; first name Jakob; place Eyweiler, Zabern; list Preußen 630; Reserve-Infanterie-Regiment 59; status *verwundet*; and GOV place identifier `EYWLERJN38NU`. The GOV value identifies the locality; it is not a military service or personal identification number.

## Refined 1916 wound reconstruction

### Inferred approach to the Galician front

The regimental history’s printed pages 146–151 materially narrow the landscape account. It records the railway route through Radziwilischki, Grodno, Białystok, Brest-Litovsk, Chełm, Lemberg and Stryj; arrival at the Bożyków and Litwinów unloading stations on the fourth day; a roughly 50 km march across the ridge between the Złota Lipa and Koropiec watersheds; entry into burning Monasterzyska; and fighting around Hrehorów and Olesza. Jacques independently remembers a multiday boxcar journey, a slow mountain or hill railway, a station outside town, an uphill march, a well-kept but half-destroyed settlement, protective ridges, crop fields, woods and trenches.

The combined synthesis therefore adds four qualified points: Radviliškis as the likely spoken-name identification; Litwinów–Bożyków as the documented unit railhead; Monasterzyska as the probable damaged hill town; and Hrehorów–Olesza as the probable early-July combat sector. These points describe the regiment’s documented route with a close oral match. They do not prove Jacques’s individual presence without a company roll, military pass or medical record. Local evidence copies are [printed p. 146](assets/rir59_1916_page_154.jpg), [p. 147](assets/rir59_1916_page_155.jpg), [p. 148](assets/rir59_1916_page_156.jpg), [p. 149](assets/rir59_1916_page_157.jpg), [p. 150](assets/rir59_1916_page_158.jpg), and [p. 151](assets/rir59_1916_page_159.jpg).

Hellmuth Neumann's 1927 *Die Geschichte des Reserve-Infanterie-Regiments Nr. 59 im Weltkriege*, compiled from official documents and reports by participants, substantially narrows the military geography. RIR 59 fought from the Strypa to the Zlota Lipa in July and August 1916. Its I Battalion lost about 200 men near Korosziatyn on 10 August and nearly 500 men south of Horozanka on 31 August. The regiment then withdrew at night toward Swistelniti and the Narajowka on 5–7 September.

The 31 August Horozanka action is the leading battle-based hypothesis for Jakob's wound because it was the regiment's largest immediately preceding loss event and occurred eleven days before the casualty notice was published. It is not proven: the casualty entry does not name Jakob's company or battalion, and Jacques remembered being wounded at night while lying in an apple orchard. The 5–7 September Narajowka withdrawal is therefore shown separately as an alternate window. See the [complete digitized regimental history](https://www.leo-bw.de/fr/web/guest/detail/-/Detail/details/DOKUMENT/wlb_digitalisate/digibib-bsz5005588845/Die%2BGeschichte%2Bdes%2BReserve-Infanterie-Regiments%2BNr%2B59%2Bim%2BWeltkriege%2B1914%2B-%2B1918%2Bnach%2Bamtlichen%2BUnterlagen%2Bund%2BBerichten%2B), especially [printed page 166](https://digital.wlb-stuttgart.de/content/urn:nbn:de:bsz:24-digibib-bsz5005588845/jpg/default/00000174.jpg) through [printed page 170](https://digital.wlb-stuttgart.de/content/urn:nbn:de:bsz:24-digibib-bsz5005588845/jpg/default/00000178.jpg).

The Bundesarchiv guidance adds an important preservation fact: for Prussian Army medical records from birth years 1891–1899, only January- and July-born soldiers were retained. Jacques/Jakob's confirmed birth date, 16 July 1895, places him inside that surviving sample, making a targeted medical-record request particularly promising.

For the Welfare Island identification, see the [New York City Department of Records history](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/records/pdf/featured-collections/almhouse-ledger-collection-1758-1952.pdf) and the [National Park Service overview of Blackwell's/Welfare/Roosevelt Island](https://home.nps.gov/places/blackwell-s-island-new-york-city.htm).

## Interactive path versions

The interactive map now offers three mutually exclusive route interpretations:

1. **Narrated locations** contains places Jacques named or described. Approximate and disputed identifications remain marked as such.
2. **Archival/unit route** contains the Jakob Schneider casualty evidence and locations documented for RIR 59 / the 1st Reserve Division. These are unit-level locations and do not prove Jakob personally occupied every point.
3. **Combined synthesis** joins oral testimony, the casualty record, the regimental history, passenger manifest, census, and supporting historical material. Every synthesis popup labels its evidence basis, supporting source, and confidence. The joining line is an interpretive chronology rather than a claim to know the exact roads, railways, sea track, or battlefield movements.

The path selector uses mutually exclusive radio buttons so alternative reconstructions are not mistaken for three simultaneous journeys. WWI front lines remain independent overlays.

The interactive Jacques map also includes toggleable, simplified WWI battle-front overlays: the stabilized Western Front (1915–16), Eastern Front (March 1916), maximum Russian advance during the Brusilov Offensive (September 1916), and Italian Front (1915–17). These are theater-scale orientation lines based on the [U.S. Military Academy Department of History WWI campaign atlas](https://dhc.westpoint.edu/atlases/WorldWarOne/) and are not trench-level GIS reconstructions.
