# English translations of the RIR 59 book excerpts

These are English translations of the **selected German passages cited by the interactive map**, not translations of every word on each scanned page. The source is Hellmuth Neumann, *Die Geschichte des Reserve-Infanterie-Regiments Nr. 59 im Weltkriege 1914–1918* (Berlin, 1927). Printed page numbers are used below; the local image filenames have different sequence numbers.

Historical place names are retained where useful, with modern or commonly used forms in parentheses. The translations aim to preserve the military meaning while using natural English. They are working research translations, not certified translations. The book describes the regiment and its battalions; it does not identify Jakob Schneider in these passages.

## Printed page 146 — rail journey to Galicia

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_154.jpg)

> At Radziwilischki (Radviliškis) we turned sharply left and continued by way of Koschedary and Landwarowo to Grodno, where rations were issued. During their retreat, the Russians had blown up the bridge over the Niemen; the railway crossed a new bridge built by German pioneers. The following day we travelled by way of Białystok to Brest-Litovsk. There the train turned, not toward Kowel as expected, but toward Chełm. We passed through Lemberg (Lviv), the capital of Galicia.

## Printed page 147 — unloading stations and Galician terrain

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_155.jpg)

> By way of Stryj, on the fourth day of our journey we reached our destination, the unloading stations of Bożyków and Litwinów. German officers received us and quartered us in the various small settlements nearby. The front ran along the ridge between the Koropiec and the Strypa. The terrain in this region is strongly hilly and crossed by fairly deeply cut watercourses, which generally flow north to south toward the Dniester. About fifteen kilometres west of the Strypa lay the Koropiec valley with the towns of Podhajce and Monasterzyska; roughly another ten kilometres west lay the Złota Lipa, with Brzeżany and Litwinów.

## Printed page 148 — march toward Monasterzyska

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_156.jpg)

> At about noon on 5 July our regiment received orders to march to Monasterzyska that afternoon. The distance was about fifty kilometres. The march began at 4 p.m. in tropical heat. As far as Zadorów the route followed the Złota Lipa; then, on a narrow road, the ridge between the Złota Lipa and the Koropiec had to be crossed. Refugees and Austrian columns made the night march more difficult.

## Printed page 149 — arrival at Monasterzyska and the July sector

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_157.jpg)

> During the night the first elements of our regiment arrived in burning Monasterzyska. The noise of battle ahead of us had fallen silent. Reserve Infantry Regiment No. 18 had arrived at the last moment and prevented a Russian breakthrough. On 7 July, during renewed Russian attacks, I Battalion moved to the area near the northern exit of Hrehorów. On 12 July the expected attack by strong Russian forces began against the front between the villages of Olesza and Hrehorów.

## Printed pages 150–151 — fighting between Olesza and Hrehorów

[View printed page 150](assets/rir59_1916_page_158.jpg) · [view printed page 151](assets/rir59_1916_page_159.jpg)

> Russian artillery prepared the attack against the positions between Olesza and Hrehorów. Two platoons of the regiment’s 3rd Company moved into the forward line of Reserve Infantry Regiment No. 3 and helped repel the attacking enemy. At 6 p.m. the 1st and 2nd Companies were sent forward from Hrehorów and inserted between battalions of Reserve Infantry Regiment No. 1. Olesza and the Austrian position north of it had meanwhile been lost, but the village and the position were retaken with strong artillery support.

## Printed page 166 — Korosziatyn and Horozanka

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_174.jpg)

> On 9 August I Battalion, together with a machine-gun platoon, marched by way of Monasterzyska to Korosziatyn. During the Russian attack on the following day, neighbouring Austrian troops gave way. After a counterattack and the intervention of cavalry, the position could no longer be held. The battalion’s losses amounted to two officers and about 200 men. On the evening of 30 August the battalion relieved Imperial and Royal Infantry Regiment No. 78 south of Horozanka.

## Printed page 167 — battle south of Horozanka

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_175.jpg)

> The 1st, 2nd and 4th Companies occupied the forward position, with the 3rd Company in reserve. Early the next day Russian artillery and mortars opened fire. At about 1:30 p.m. Russian infantry attacked. The 1st and 2nd Companies repelled the assault, but the 1st Company was enveloped and overwhelmed. Another strong attack at about 4 p.m. was initially beaten back. On the left, however, Reserve Infantry Regiment No. 18 faced similar pressure, and Russian troops broke through adjoining Austrian positions.

## Printed page 168 — nearly 500 battalion losses

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_176.jpg)

> Reserve Infantry Regiment No. 18 had to abandon its position. Our battalion received no word of this and, at about 4 p.m., was enveloped from the left. The withdrawal began under pressure from the rapidly advancing enemy. The losses were severe and amounted to almost 500 men. Those who had not been killed or captured could save only their bare lives from the threatened encirclement. As a result, the battalion temporarily had no further combat value.

## Printed page 169 — order to withdraw

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_177.jpg)

> On 5 September renewed heavy attacks were directed chiefly against the Imperial and Royal 12th Division south of the 1st Reserve Division, and that division was almost destroyed. The position could no longer be held. During the evening the brigade reported that a withdrawal would probably take place during the night of 5–6 September. At 11 p.m. the forward positions were still ordered to hold.

## Printed page 170 — Narajówka line

[View the German scan](assets/rir59_1916_page_178.jpg)

> A divisional order arriving at 2 a.m., issued on higher authority, ordered the general withdrawal toward Świstelniki on the Narajówka. Strong rear guards were initially to remain on a line about six kilometres behind the existing front in order to delay the Russian advance as long as possible. Disengagement from the enemy took place during the night without interference. By the morning of 6 September the II and I Battalions had reached Świstelniki; III Battalion arrived during the evening. The regiment’s new position extended along the east bank of the Narajówka from south of Świstelniki to Height 349.
