Jakob Schneider archival match

A German casualty-list entry closely matches Jacques’s name, likely home village, regiment, theaters of service, and reported wound during the Brusilov Offensive.

Likely 1916 rail evacuation

The teal overlay separates the documented railway geography from Jacques's unlocated first hospital. Its train marker at Świstelniki/Svitanok is the leading candidate for a loading or collection point because the railway crossed the Narajówka sector occupied by RIR 59 on 5–7 September 1916. The highlighted corridor then follows a plausible pre-1914 railway connection west through Lviv, Przemyśl and Kraków into Upper Silesia and onward toward Breslau/Wrocław.

Evidence limit: the stations and railway network are historical; Jacques's use of this particular railhead, junction sequence, and destination hospital is inferred. No located hospital register or transport list yet supplies his train number or exact route.

Why the match is compelling

  1. Name: an Alsatian Jacques could appear as Jakob in Imperial German records.
  2. Home: Eyweiler is modern Eywiller near Saverne. Its spoken name may explain the transcript’s uncertain “Aviaire.”
  3. Unit route: RIR 59 belonged to the 1st Reserve Division, which served in East Prussia and the Baltic/Eastern Front before moving to Galicia during the Brusilov Offensive.
  4. Timing: RIR 59 fought from the Strypa to the Zlota Lipa and Narajowka in July–September 1916. Its I Battalion suffered nearly 500 casualties near Horozanka on 31 August; the casualty entry was published September 11.
  5. Context: Alsatian-Lorrain soldiers were frequently directed to the Eastern Front.

What the record does not prove

The list gives no birth date, parents, occupation, service number, company, exact wound date, or hospital. Its publication date may lag the injury. Unit movements do not prove that Jakob personally accompanied every movement shown on the map.

Best confirmation records

  • Eywiller civil birth records and parents’ names.
  • The continuation page of the 1922 passenger manifest, which may add his U.S. contact, ticket, money, prior-U.S.-residence, birthplace, and physical-description fields.
  • Alsatian veteran-card or pension files.
  • German military hospital records. His exact date, 16 July 1895, is now known and falls within the Bundesarchiv's surviving July-born sample for the 1891–1899 birth years.

Military-career reconstruction

NEW PRIMARY UNIT SOURCE

Hellmuth Neumann's 1927 history of Reserve-Infanterie-Regiment 59 was compiled from official documents and reports by participants. Its sequence—East Prussia, Poland, Lithuania, Dünaburg, Galicia and Riga—closely matches Jacques's recollections. The history documents these RIR 59 operations immediately before the 11 September casualty-list notice:

  • 5 July–7 August: Strypa, Baryszka and Koropiec sectors.
  • 7–15 August: withdrawal toward the Zlota Lipa.
  • 14–30 August: positions along the Zlota Lipa.
  • 10 August: I Battalion lost about 200 men near Korosziatyn.
  • 31 August: I Battalion was enveloped south of Horozanka and lost almost 500 men, leaving it temporarily without combat value. This is the strongest battle-based wound hypothesis.
  • 5–7 September: the regiment withdrew at night toward Swistelniti and occupied a new line along the Narajowka. This is the main alternate window because Jacques remembered being wounded at night.

Important: the history documents the regiment and its battalions, not Jakob personally. His company and battalion remain unknown. The map therefore displays Horozanka as a leading hypothesis and the Narajowka withdrawal as an alternate—not as established personal stops.

Immigration record

MANIFEST IDENTIFIED

The FamilySearch/NARA manifest identifies Jacques Schneider, age 26, on list line 18 of the S.S. La Lorraine, sailing from Le Havre on 18 February 1922. His age precisely fits the confirmed birth date of 16 July 1895. The row records him as a single French waiter, last resident in Paris; it names friend Mr. Jaeger, 3 rue des Francs Bourgeois, Paris, and gives Newark as his intended final destination.

This corrects the earlier March 1923 theory. The St. Patrick’s Day passage in the oral history cannot be used as an exact next-day arrival date. The primary manifest is stronger evidence. The downloaded sheet gives the sailing date but does not display the New York arrival date or landing pier, so neither is stated as certain here.

How the new record changes the earlier search

  • Identity: exact given and family names, fitting age, French nationality, Paris residence, waiter occupation, and a Newark destination collectively make this an exceptionally strong match.
  • Chronology: the 18 February 1922 sailing is documentary evidence; the previously inferred 16 March 1923 arrival and speculative Bruckner lead are superseded.
  • Geography: Newark was the stated intended destination, while Jacques recalled first taking a taxi to East 49th Street. These can coexist: a manifest destination is an intention, not proof of the first address actually used after disembarkation.

1950 census record

The supplied 1950 census sheet places the family in West Belmar, Monmouth County, New Jersey, enumeration district 13-261, sheet 13, dwelling 135. Line 28 lists Jacques Schneider, age 54, born in France, married, and working 40 hours as a bartender. The household continues with wife Justine, age 48, and son Harry, age 25, both born in New York. The written house number appears to be 927; the street name is inherited from an earlier line and is not visible on this sheet, so the map retains “Curtis Avenue” from the oral history without claiming that the census itself prints it here.
